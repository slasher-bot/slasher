# Where an entry's body says the same thing as its title, keep the title alone

**Trigger:** When drafting a journal entry

Where the body of a journal entry would be identical or nearly identical to its `# <title>`, omit the body. The entry is then the title heading, and a `**Tags:**` line where it has one. No prose repeats what the title already says.

This is the title-and-body counterpart of the journal-entries-are-recorded-in-the-writers-journal-directory-one-file-per-entry rule, which omits the `**Summary:**` line where it would duplicate the body. Together they keep one sentence from appearing in the title, the summary, and the body alike.

**Why:** it avoids redundancy. A one-line entry whose title already says everything does not need the same words again below it.

**How to apply:** after drafting an entry, compare the title and the body. Where the body adds no fact, no detail, and no framing beyond the title, drop the body and keep the title.

## Compressed

1. Where a journal entry's body would be identical or nearly identical to its `# <title>`, omit the body.
2. The entry is then the title heading, and a `**Tags:**` line where it has one.
3. No prose repeats what the title already says. Omit the `**Summary:**` line where it would duplicate the body, as the journal-entries-are-recorded-in-the-writers-journal-directory-one-file-per-entry rule directs.
4. After drafting an entry, compare the title and the body.
5. Where the body adds no fact, no detail, and no framing beyond the title, drop the body and keep the title.
